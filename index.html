Cell theory

Cell theory is one of the most important ideas in biology. It explains how cells are connected to all living things.

The Three Main Principles

  • All living things are made of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in living things.
  • All cells come from pre-existing cells.

Unicellular and Multicellular Organisms

A unicellular organism is made of only one cell. Bacteria and many microscopic organisms are unicellular.

A multicellular organism is made of many cells. Humans, animals, and plants are multicellular organisms.

Different Cells Have Different Jobs

Cells can have different shapes and functions. For example, nerve cells carry messages, muscle cells help the body move, and red blood cells carry oxygen.

Plant and Animal Cells

Plant and animal cells have some parts in common, such as a nucleus, cytoplasm, and a cell membrane.

Plant cells also have a cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole. These parts help plants stay firm and make food using sunlight.

How Do New Cells Form?

New cells form when existing cells divide. Cell division helps organisms grow, replace old cells, and repair damaged tissues.

Why Is Cell Theory Important?

Cell theory helps scientists understand how living things grow, reproduce, and carry out life processes. It is also important in medicine because many diseases affect the way cells work.
